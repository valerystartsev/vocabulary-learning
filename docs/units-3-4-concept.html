Units 3 & 4 — Money, Banking & Measuring the Economy

From cash and central banks to GDP and balance sheets. 12 interactive features built around the official exam vocabulary, designed to teach the same concepts faster than the printed textbook.

A1–A2 CEFR Russian-speaking economics students React 18 · Vite · Supabase

Course progression · 4 units

From micro to macro

Each unit builds on the previous. Unit 1 zooms in on a single market. Unit 2 zooms into the firm operating inside it. Unit 3 zooms out to the financial system that surrounds the firm. Unit 4 zooms out to the whole economy. The same scaffolding repeats in every unit so students always know where they are.

Unit 3 · Money, Banking & Monetary Policy

7 features · 28 terms
Feature 3.1

Money Functions Compass

Four equal functions, not a linear chain — exactly how the textbook frames them

Required: Money — Role, Forms, Functions

In the textbook (3.4.3) students must identify which function of money applies to each life situation: Jane collecting her salary, Mr. Collins comparing prices, the supermarket accepting dollars. The compass turns the same exercise into a spatial map — students click any sector and immediately see definition + Russian translation + a real example. The 2×2 layout makes the four functions feel parallel, not sequential.

Interactive · click any sector

средство обмена

Money you use to trade for goods and services instead of barter.

Jane pays $50 for groceries.

Store of value

средство сбережения

Money keeps its worth over time — you can save it for the future.

Jane puts $15 into her savings account.

Standard of value

мера стоимости

Money lets you compare prices of completely different things.

A car costs 50× more than a phone.

Near money

квазиденьги

Things that act like money but aren't cash — savings, bonds, credit cards.

A demand deposit you can withdraw any day.

Sentence Builder · preview
Money serves as a store of value when you save it.
Why it beats 3.4.3 in the book: the textbook asks students to label each scenario from memory. The compass shows definitions next to each other — students compare the 4 functions instead of recalling them. A spatial 2×2 reads cleaner on mobile than a linear list and trains exam-grade discrimination between store of value vs standard of value.
Feature 3.2

Forms of Money Spectrum

Beads → coins → fiat → digital — timeline of what money has been

Required: Forms of money

The textbook (page 168) lists historical forms in dense prose: "beads, shells, dog's teeth, cattle, stones, tobacco, fishhooks, slaves, gold, silver…" — students gloss over it. The spectrum turns the same list into a clickable timeline. Students see what each era used + why it failed → why we ended up with fiat money. This sets up the key textbook distinction between fiat money (state-backed) and near money (substitutes).

Feature 3.4

Central Bank Functions Wheel

All six functions in one grid — covers exercise 3.5.3 completely

Required: Central bank functions

Exercise 3.5.3 in the textbook is a gap-fill paragraph about central banks where students must drop in 12 words from a box. The result: students complete the gap but don't remember the six functions afterward. The wheel shows all six as parallel cards — students can study, compare, and reference them at any time. The bottom row reinforces the four banks the textbook names: Bank of England, Federal Reserve, Bundesbank, Bank of Russia.

Six functions of a central bank
Issue currency

эмитировать деньги

Print banknotes and mint coins — the country's legal tender.

Control money supply

контролировать денежную массу

Increase or decrease the amount of money in circulation.

Implement monetary policy

проводить денежно-кредитную политику

Set interest rates to influence demand, output, and inflation.

Lender of last resort

кредитор последней инстанции

Lend money to commercial banks in liquidity crises.

Supervise banking system

надзор за банками

Make sure commercial banks operate safely and legally.

Impose reserve requirements

резервные требования

Force commercial banks to keep a % of deposits as reserves.

The textbook names these central banks: 🇬🇧 Bank of England 🇺🇸 Federal Reserve (Fed) 🇩🇪 Bundesbank 🇷🇺 Bank of Russia
Sentence Builder · central bank
By decreasing the money supply, the central bank makes money tight.
What this fixes: exercise 3.5.3 in the textbook is a 12-blank gap-fill where students search for the right word. The wheel inverts this — students see the answers first, structured. Gap-fill becomes review, not discovery. On the exam, the question "name 3 functions of a central bank" gets answered with confidence because students remember the grid position, not just the word.
Feature 3.5

Bank Account Picker

Filter by need → see which account fits — covers 3.6 dialogue completely

Required: Types of bank accounts

In dialogue 3.6 the textbook lists 6 client situations (a teen inheriting $15k, an elderly couple with $400, a student getting a research grant…) and asks "what kind of bank account would each open?". The picker turns this into a filter UI: students click a need (long-term saving / daily spending / first account / earning interest) and see which account types match — exactly the decision flow the textbook tests.

Feature 3.6

Required: USA, Russia, Canada, UK, France, Germany, Japan

Section 3.7 "Reading for Cross-cultural Associations" gives prose passages about British and Canadian banking. The map extends this to the 7 countries on your required list. Each country has the same data structure — central bank, currency, dominant account types, banking culture — so students can compare across cultures. This is the spiritual successor to the EconomicWorld page in Unit 2, but for Unit 3's banking domain.

90% of British adults have a bank account. The British financial system is driven by ordinary citizens who save about 15% of their income. About 20% of the population own shares. Feature 3.7

Loan Simulator — Arthur's Car

The textbook's Case Study, but you can actually play with the numbers

Required: Loans, borrowing money

Section 3.8 Case Study tells the story of Arthur, a student who wants to buy a car and visits a bank loan officer. The textbook explains two options — overdraft vs personal loan — in prose. The simulator lets students actually try Arthur's choices: change the loan amount, interest rate, repayment period — watch the monthly payment update live. Vocabulary lights up in context (principal, interest, instalment, overdraft).

Feature 4.1

GDP Calculator · Two Approaches

Earnings-and-cost vs flow-of-product — same number, two ways

Required by textbook 4.5

The textbook explains both approaches in two prose paragraphs (page 270). Most students understand each separately but don't see why they give the same total. The calculator forces the realisation: every dollar of income (wages + rent + interest + profit) = every dollar of spending (households + investment + government + net exports). Change one column, the other updates. The verdict bar confirms when they match.

Both approaches match. Every dollar of income equals a dollar of spending — that's the circular flow of the economy.
What the textbook can't do: it states the equivalence as a fact. Students take it on faith. The calculator proves it by letting students try and fail — change one input, totals diverge, verdict bar turns red. This is the single highest-impact feature in Unit 4, because the "two approaches give one answer" idea is the most-tested and most-misunderstood concept on the exam.

Feature 4.4

Balance Sheet Builder

Assets = Liabilities + Equity — the equation that must always balance

Required: assets, equity, liability

The textbook lists asset, liability, equity as vocab without anchoring them to the accounting equation. The builder makes the equation tangible — students enter numbers, the equation checks itself live. When it doesn't balance, an explanation appears. This is the foundation for the Annual Report feature that comes next.

Wrap-up

Final project

Capstone Project · end of course

Analyse a real Russian company

The final project ties the whole course together. Students pick a real Russian company — Sber, Yandex, X5, Norilsk Nickel — download a public annual report, fill in a structured template (assets, liabilities, equity, leading indicators, where the company is in the business cycle), and write a 200-word executive summary in English. Submission goes through the existing teacher dashboard.

1
Pick a company

From a curated list of 8 Russian public companies. Each has an English annual report.

2
Fill the template

Assets, liabilities, equity, revenue, recent performance. Uses Unit 3 + 4 vocabulary directly.

3
Diagnose the cycle

Identify which business cycle phase the company is in, using indicators.

4
Write 200-word summary

Executive-style English summary. Auto-checked for required vocabulary use.
